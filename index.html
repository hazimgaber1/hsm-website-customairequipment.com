    Why custom

    The jobs we get called for.

    Most of our projects start after a catalog selection has already failed. These are the constraints we hear most often, and how we build around them.

    • The only way in is a 36-inch door.

      Knockdown construction

      Units ship as panels, coils, and fan modules sized for your doorway or freight elevator. Our crew rebuilds them in place and leak-tests the casing before startup.

    • The new unit has to land on the old curb.

      Footprint matching

      We survey the existing unit and match its footprint, duct connections, and service clearances, so the replacement bolts up without new steel or roof work.

    • The room next door has to stay at NC 25.

      Acoustic design

      Fan arrays, sound attenuators, and perforated liners, selected against a noise criteria target with sound data measured in our own lab.

    • It's −40° outside, or 120° inside the plant.

      Extreme ambients

      Thermally broken casings, preheat and freeze protection, and motors and bearings rated for the conditions the unit will actually see.

    • The airstream is wet, corrosive, or full of dust.

      Materials and wheel design

      Stainless and coated construction, radial wheels for material handling, and spark-resistant builds to AMCA 99-0401 Type A, B, or C.

    • The air can't stop, even for maintenance.

      Redundancy

      N+1 fan arrays with isolation dampers let your team pull a fan while the unit keeps running. Dual coils and redundant controls where the space demands it.

    From first call to startup.

    One application engineer stays with your project from selection through startup, so the person answering your questions knows the unit.
